Editorial Standards

How field notes are researched, sourced, reviewed, and corrected — published so readers can hold the platform to it.

These standards apply to everything published here. They are public so that a reader can check whether we have met them.

Sourcing

We summarise; we do not scoop. Field notes draw on investigations that already exist in the public record — the Sunday Times, the Guardian, Reuters, AP, the New York Times, the Bureau of Investigative Journalism, peer-reviewed studies, parliamentary inquiries, and regulatory filings.

The original publisher is always credited, by name, in the note itself. Where a link exists, it is given. The standing list of who we cite is at /sources/.

Primary instruments over coverage of them. Where a claim rests on a regulation, a ruling, or a filing, we cite that document rather than an article about it.

Numbers

We do not publish a statistic we cannot attribute to a named body and a dated publication.

This rules out a good deal of widely circulated material. Where a figure exists but is contested or its basis is unclear, we give the figure, name its source, and describe the limitation rather than dropping it silently.

Uncertainty

Where the evidence does not support a clean conclusion, the note says so. Where two credible bodies read the same standard differently, we describe both readings rather than adopting one quietly.

The tools carry the same discipline. The Record Checker holds only what the public record shows, and says on its face that absence of a record is not evidence of good practice.

Imagery

The archive's visual essays are AI-generated, directed by the founder, and conceptual rather than documentary. This is disclosed on the archive and in the About page.

No image on this platform is presented as photographic evidence of a specific event, and none should be read as such.

Review

Datasets — the decoders, the record, the directory, the reading list — each declare when they were last reviewed and how often they should be. When one passes its cycle, the page says so on its face rather than ageing quietly. That notice is generated at build time from the data file's own metadata; no editor has to remember to add it.

Corrections

We correct errors of fact promptly and visibly.

Substantive corrections — where an error changes the meaning — are noted on the entry and listed at /corrections/ with the date, the error, and the correction.

Minor corrections — typography, broken links, formatting — are made without notice.

We do not silently delete. A withdrawn note is marked as withdrawn with an explanation, and its URL continues to resolve.

To report an error, write to corrections@feral-femme.co with the entry, the passage, and the basis for the correction.

Independence

No advertisers. No industry sponsorship. No affiliate links. No sponsored placements. No funding from any company whose conduct this platform documents.

The platform is funded by reader donations, and that is the only revenue it takes.

AI use

Language models are used in producing this platform — for drafting, structuring, and checking internal consistency, and for the archive's editorial imagery.

They are not used as a source of fact. Every factual claim, citation, date, and reference is verified against the named source by a human before publication. A model's assertion that a report says something is not evidence that it does, and is never treated as such.

We state this because the alternative — using these tools and not saying so — is precisely the practice this platform exists to document elsewhere.
