The remit

Industries We Document

Ten industries, one pattern. Each is built somewhere on a body that could not agree to it. For each we keep a standing reference — the single most useful public investigation or database in that field — alongside our own field notes.

01Beauty

BeautyAnimal Testing

Standing reference

Humane Society International · ongoing campaign

#BeCrueltyFree — Country-by-Country Cosmetics Testing Tracker

The most comprehensive public tracker of which jurisdictions have banned cosmetics testing on animals, where loopholes still exist, and which bills are in motion. Updated continuously as policy changes. Essential reference work for anyone trying to verify a “cruelty-free” claim against the law that actually applies.

Read at HSI

02Fashion

FashionWomen's RightsLabour

Standing reference

The Sunday Times · 2020 · Boohoo Leicester investigation

Inside Britain’s Sweatshops: Boohoo, Leicester, and Sub-Minimum-Wage Garment Work

The undercover reporting that documented Leicester garment factories paying workers under UK minimum wage to supply Boohoo. Triggered the Garment and Textiles Workers Trust review and remains the most consequential UK garment investigation of the last decade.

Read at The Times

Also worth reading

Synthetics Anonymous — Changing Markets

03Food & Agriculture

FoodLabour

Standing reference

The New York Times · 2023 · Hannah Dreier

Alone and Exploited: Migrant Children in US Meatpacking

Pulitzer-winning investigation documenting unaccompanied migrant children working in US slaughterhouses and food-processing plants, often through subcontracted cleaning agencies. Led to Department of Labor enforcement actions and a federal interagency taskforce.

Read at NYT

Also worth reading

Compassion in World Farming — research reports

Open

04Animal Welfare

AnimalsWildlife

Standing reference

World Animal Protection · 2020

The Hidden Cost of Wildlife Tourism

Audit of wildlife tourism attractions globally — elephant rides, tiger selfies, dolphin shows. WAP’s scoring methodology ranks each attraction type on welfare impact, with detailed field documentation. Triggered changes in TripAdvisor’s listing policies and several tour-operator commitments.

Read at WAP

Also worth reading

Born Free — zoo investigations

Open

05Women’s Rights

WomenLabour

Standing reference

SOMO & India Committee of the Netherlands · multiple reports

Captured by Cotton: The Sumangali Scheme in Tamil Nadu Garment Mills

Years of documentation on the bonded-labour scheme targeting young women in South Indian spinning mills — multi-year contracts, lump-sum payments framed as dowry, confined accommodation. Names supplier factories and the major brands sourcing from them.

Read at SOMO

Also worth reading

Geena Davis Institute — research

06Environment

EnvironmentGreenwashing

Standing reference

The Guardian, Die Zeit & SourceMaterial · 2023

Revealed: More Than 90% of Rainforest Carbon Offsets by the Biggest Provider Are Worthless

The investigation that broke open the voluntary carbon market. Analysed Verra’s rainforest credit programme and found a substantial majority of credits did not represent real emissions reductions. The fallout reshaped corporate offset strategies worldwide.

Read at The Guardian

Also worth reading

Greenpeace — Detox

Open

07Technology

TechLabourMining

Standing reference

Amnesty International · 2016, updated

“This Is What We Die For”: Human Rights Abuses in the DRC Cobalt Trade

The foundational report mapping artisanal cobalt mining in the Democratic Republic of Congo to global consumer-electronics supply chains. Documented child labour, fatal accidents, and severe health consequences, and named the brands sourcing from the affected trade.

Read at Amnesty

Also worth reading

Out of Control — Forbrukerrådet

08Consumer Rights

ConsumerGreenwashing

Standing reference

UK Competition and Markets Authority · 2022–ongoing

Green Claims Code — Investigations into ASOS, Boohoo, Asda and Unilever

The CMA’s enforcement portfolio against misleading environmental claims. The 2022 ASOS / Boohoo / Asda case forced commitments to substantiate or remove sustainability claims. The 2023 Unilever investigation extended the same scrutiny to FMCG.

Read at GOV.UK

Also worth reading

Deceived by Design — Forbrukerrådet

Open

09Corporate Accountability

CorporateSupply Chains

Standing reference

Business & Human Rights Resource Centre · ongoing

Allegations Tracker — Corporate Human Rights Abuses

The single most useful open database in this space. Tracks alleged corporate human-rights abuses and each company’s response — or refusal to respond. Searchable by company, country, industry, and issue. If a brand has been named in a major investigation, it is here.

Read at BHRRC

Not Fit-for-Purpose — MSI Integrity

10Media & Advertising

MediaInfluencers

Standing reference

UK Advertising Standards Authority & CMA · 2021 sweep

An Influencer’s Guide — Disclosure Compliance Monitoring

The 2021 monitoring sweep documented widespread non-disclosure of paid promotion across UK influencer Instagram and TikTok accounts. The ASA followed up with public rulings against named accounts, and the accompanying Influencer’s Guide remains the clearest statement of what disclosure actually requires.

Read at the ASA
