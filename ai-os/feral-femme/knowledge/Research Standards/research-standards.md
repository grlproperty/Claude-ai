# Research and sourcing standards

## Sourcing
- Field notes draw on investigations already in the public record: the Sunday Times, the Guardian, Reuters, AP, the New York Times, the Bureau of Investigative Journalism, peer-reviewed studies, parliamentary inquiries and regulatory filings.
- The original publisher is always credited by name in the note. Where a link exists, it is given.
- Primary instruments over coverage of them: where a claim rests on a regulation, a ruling or a filing, cite that document.

## Numbers
No statistic is published without a named body and a dated publication. Where a figure is contested or its basis unclear, give the figure, name its source and describe the limitation.

## Evidence classification
Every claim is one of: **established fact**, **allegation**, **finding**, **opinion**, **editorial interpretation**, **unresolved question**. An allegation is never presented as an established finding, and it names who alleges it.

## AI use
Language models are used for drafting, structuring and checking internal consistency, and for the archive's imagery. **They are not used as a source of fact.** Every factual claim, citation, date and reference is verified against the named source by a human before publication. A model's assertion that a report says something is not evidence that it does.

## Corrections
Errors of fact are corrected promptly and visibly. Substantive corrections are noted on the entry and listed on the corrections page with the date, the error and the correction. Minor corrections are made without notice. Nothing is silently deleted: a withdrawn note is marked withdrawn and its address keeps working. Reports go to corrections@feral-femme.co.

## Review
Each tool dataset declares when it was last reviewed and how often it should be. A dataset past its cycle says so on its own face.
