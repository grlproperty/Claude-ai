This platform does not tell you what to buy. It does describe, accurately, what changes outcomes — and the ordering below is by leverage, which is not the same as the ordering by visibility.

## The highest-leverage action is unglamorous

Every module in this curriculum ends at the same constraint: **validation capacity**.

The scientific literature contains far more promising non-animal methods than the validation pipeline can process. Between a method demonstrated in a laboratory and a method a regulator will accept lies a formal, multi-laboratory validation process run by bodies such as EURL ECVAM, ICCVAM, and JaCVAM. It takes years and is under-resourced relative to the scale of the task.

This matters because it changes what a legislator can be asked for. A demand for prohibition can be refused on the grounds that alternatives are not available for every endpoint — and for repeated-dose toxicity that refusal is currently defensible. A request to fund the process that makes alternatives available removes the grounds.

If you write to a representative about this subject, this is the ask with the highest expected value, and almost nobody makes it.

## Ask the four questions

When a brand makes a cruelty-free claim, ask it publicly, in the place it made the claim:

Does the claim cover ingredients, or only the finished product?

Does it extend to suppliers and contract laboratories?

Is there a fixed cut-off date, and what is it?

Do you sell in markets where an authority may require testing, and under what exemption?

These are not hostile questions. A company with a genuine commitment can answer all four in a paragraph, and many will. The pressure is created by the questions being asked consistently and in public, because an unanswered question is itself informative to everyone reading.

## Support the mechanisms that verify

Certification schemes are the only mechanism currently converting a vague marketing phrase into something checkable. Their limitations are real and set out in our [certification guide](/certifications/) — and a certified claim with a fixed cut-off date and supplier declarations is a fundamentally different kind of statement from an uncertified one.

Choosing certified products, and saying why, is what sustains the schemes.

## Be accurate in public

This is the action most likely to be dismissed as trivial, and it is not.

The case against animal testing in cosmetics is strong on the evidence. It is routinely damaged by overstatement — a global figure with no traceable source, a claim that alternatives exist for everything, an assertion that the EU ban is total. Each of those is easy for an informed opponent to correct, and each correction costs the argument more than the original claim gained.

Sharing the accurate version, including the parts that are inconvenient, is a real contribution. It is also free.

## Teach it

The [learning modules](/learn/) are written to be taught and are licensed for non-commercial educational use with attribution. Slide decks and lesson plans are available through [educational licensing](/licensing/), and institutional licences fund the free tier for everyone else.

Anyone who has worked through the five modules can explain why *animal testing is banned in Europe* is an incomplete sentence. That is a more durable outcome than a single purchasing decision.
