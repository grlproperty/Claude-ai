Certification Decoder

A label is only as good as the standard behind it. Search a certification, seal, or logo you have seen on a product and find out who runs it, what it actually verifies, and — just as importantly — what it does not.

Leaping Bunny

animals

Run by Cruelty Free International

What it verifies

No animal testing at any stage — finished product AND ingredients — by the brand, its suppliers, or third parties, with a fixed cut-off date and independent audits plus supplier monitoring.

What it does not guarantee

Does NOT mean vegan. A Leaping Bunny product can still contain animal-derived ingredients (beeswax, lanolin, carmine).

Source — Cruelty Free International / Leaping Bunny Programme.

PETA — Beauty Without Bunnies

animals

Run by PETA

What it verifies

Brand has signed a statement of assurance that it and its suppliers do not test on animals. Two logos exist: ‘Animal Test-Free’ and ‘Animal Test-Free & Vegan’.

What it does not guarantee

The standard base logo relies on a signed pledge rather than independent audit, and the test-free-only logo does not mean vegan.

Source — PETA Beauty Without Bunnies programme.

Certified Vegan (Vegan Society / Vegan Action)

animals

Run by The Vegan Society (UK) / Vegan Action (US)

What it verifies

No animal-derived ingredients and no animal testing by the company. The sunflower trademark requires an application and ingredient review.

What it does not guarantee

‘Vegan’ alone (uncertified) is unregulated. Vegan does not automatically mean cruelty-free unless the certifier also covers testing.

Source — The Vegan Society Trademark standards.

RSPCA Assured

animals

Run by RSPCA (UK)

What it verifies

Farm-animal welfare against RSPCA species-specific standards, covering the animal’s life, transport, and slaughter, with assessment visits.

What it does not guarantee

It is a welfare scheme, not organic or free-range by default, and does not mean the animal was not killed.

Source — RSPCA Assured scheme standards.

Certified Humane

animals

Run by Humane Farm Animal Care

What it verifies

Farm-animal welfare standards including space, no cages for hens, and no routine antibiotics, with annual third-party inspection.

What it does not guarantee

A welfare label, not a ‘no-slaughter’ label; standards differ from organic and from country-specific free-range definitions.

Source — Humane Farm Animal Care standards.

RSPO

climate

Run by Roundtable on Sustainable Palm Oil

What it verifies

Palm oil produced to RSPO criteria on a certified plantation. ‘Segregated’ and ‘Identity Preserved’ are the strongest supply-chain models.

What it does not guarantee

Weaker models (‘Mass Balance’, ‘Book & Claim’/credits) mean certified and non-certified oil may be mixed. RSPO has faced criticism over deforestation in member supply chains.

Source — RSPO Principles & Criteria; Greenpeace critique.

GOTS

social

Run by Global Organic Textile Standard

What it verifies

Textiles with a minimum 70% certified organic fibre PLUS environmental and social criteria (banned toxic inputs, wastewater treatment, workers’ rights) across the whole supply chain.

What it does not guarantee

‘Made with organic’ (70–94%) is a lower grade than the ‘organic’ grade (95%+). Organic cotton still uses significant water.

Source — Global Organic Textile Standard v7.0.

OEKO-TEX Standard 100

social

Run by OEKO-TEX Association

What it verifies

That the finished textile has been tested for a list of harmful substances and is below defined limits — a chemical-safety label for the end product.

What it does not guarantee

It is NOT an organic, animal-welfare, labour, or environmental-footprint standard. It only addresses substances in the tested article.

Source — OEKO-TEX Standard 100 test criteria.

Fairtrade

social

Run by Fairtrade International

What it verifies

Producers received the Fairtrade Minimum Price and a Fairtrade Premium, with standards on labour rights and environmental practice, independently audited by FLOCERT.

What it does not guarantee

Covers the certified ingredient(s), not necessarily the whole product; on composite products a percentage applies. Not an organic or animal-welfare label.

Source — Fairtrade International Standards / FLOCERT.

B Corp

social

Run by B Lab

What it verifies

The whole COMPANY passed a verified assessment of its social and environmental performance, accountability, and transparency (minimum score 80), re-certified every three years.

What it does not guarantee

It rates the company overall — not any single product, and not specifically animal welfare or veganism. A B Corp can still sell products you might avoid.

Source — B Lab / B Impact Assessment.

FSC

climate

Run by Forest Stewardship Council

What it verifies

Wood, paper, or forest-derived material comes from responsibly managed forests meeting FSC environmental and social standards, tracked by chain-of-custody.

What it does not guarantee

‘FSC Mix’ blends certified, recycled, and ‘controlled’ material — not 100% certified. Look for ‘FSC 100%’ for the strongest claim.

Source — Forest Stewardship Council standards.

Soil Association Organic

food

Run by Soil Association (UK)

What it verifies

Organic certification for food, and a separate strict standard for organic health & beauty covering ingredients, GM-free, and (for beauty) no animal testing.

What it does not guarantee

Organic addresses how something is grown/made, not animal-welfare outcomes or whether an animal was killed.

Source — Soil Association Organic Standards.

USDA Organic / EU Organic Leaf

food

Run by USDA (US) / EU Commission

What it verifies

Food produced under legally-defined organic rules: no synthetic pesticides/fertilisers, no GMOs, and defined animal-welfare and feed rules for livestock.

What it does not guarantee

Organic is not automatically ‘free-range’ nor a guarantee of high welfare beyond the baseline rules; it is not a cruelty-free or vegan label.

Source — USDA NOP / EU Reg 2018/848.

Rainforest Alliance

climate

Run by Rainforest Alliance

What it verifies

Farms (coffee, cocoa, tea, bananas) met standards on forests, climate, human rights, and livelihoods, audited against the 2020 Sustainable Agriculture Standard.

What it does not guarantee

Uses mass-balance for cocoa (certified and non-certified can be mixed) and has faced scrutiny over on-the-ground enforcement.

Source — Rainforest Alliance 2020 Standard.

Fair Wear Foundation

social

Run by Fair Wear Foundation

What it verifies

That a clothing brand is a member working to improve labour conditions in its supply chain, assessed on its practices (a ‘Brand Performance Check’).

What it does not guarantee

It rates the brand’s EFFORT and management, not a pass/fail guarantee that every factory is perfect. Not an environmental or animal label.

Source — Fair Wear Foundation Code of Labour Practices.

Responsible Down / Wool Standard (RDS / RWS)

animals

Run by Textile Exchange

What it verifies

Down or wool traced through the supply chain with animal-welfare criteria — RDS prohibits live-plucking and force-feeding; RWS addresses sheep welfare and land management.

What it does not guarantee

It improves welfare within animal use; it does not mean no animal was farmed or slaughtered. Mulesing is prohibited under RWS but check the specific claim.

Source — Textile Exchange RDS / RWS standards.

Global Recycled Standard (GRS)

environment

Run by Textile Exchange

What it verifies

Tracks recycled content through the supply chain (minimum 20% to carry the logo, 50%+ for full claims), plus social and environmental criteria at each stage.

What it does not guarantee

Does NOT mean the whole garment is recycled, or that it is low-impact overall — only that the certified recycled content is genuine and traceable.

Source — Textile Exchange GRS.

bluesign

environment

Run by bluesign technologies

What it verifies

Manages chemical inputs across textile manufacturing — restricting hazardous substances and improving resource use at the factory level.

What it does not guarantee

Does NOT address animal welfare or garment-worker wages; it is a chemical and environmental management system, not an ethics-of-labour mark.

Source — bluesign system.

Cradle to Cradle Certified

environment

Run by Cradle to Cradle Products Innovation Institute

What it verifies

Assesses products across material health, reuse, renewable energy, water, and social fairness, at Bronze–Platinum levels.

What it does not guarantee

A low tier (Bronze) is a much weaker claim than Gold/Platinum — the level matters, and the logo alone doesn’t tell you which.

Source — Cradle to Cradle Products Innovation Institute.

Fair Trade Certified (US)

social

Run by Fair Trade USA

What it verifies

Sets standards for wages, safe conditions, and community funds for producers; distinct from Fairtrade International (different standards and governance).

What it does not guarantee

Does NOT certify the whole company — only specific certified products or facilities; the rest of the brand may be unaudited.

Source — Fair Trade USA.

ZQ Merino

animals

Run by The New Zealand Merino Company

What it verifies

Wool standard covering animal welfare (including non-mulesing), environmental management, and social responsibility on accredited farms.

What it does not guarantee

Applies to the certified wool fibre, not the finished garment’s full supply chain (dyeing, sewing, labour elsewhere).

Source — ZQ / NZ Merino.

Regenerative Organic Certified (ROC)

environment

Run by Regenerative Organic Alliance

What it verifies

Builds on organic certification with added requirements for soil health, animal welfare, and farmworker fairness — one of the stricter farm-level marks.

What it does not guarantee

Still farm-level: it certifies how a raw material was grown, not how the final product was manufactured or by whom.

Source — Regenerative Organic Alliance.
